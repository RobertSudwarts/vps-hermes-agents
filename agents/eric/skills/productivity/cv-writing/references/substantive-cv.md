# Substantive CV drafting

Use for a new role-specific CV, an open application, or a rewrite that changes the hiring argument.

1. Read the actual role specification and identify the work, essential requirements, and material uncertainties. For an open application, identify a bounded contribution supported by verified employer context; do not invent a vacancy or title.
2. Read only the relevant canonical career sources and confirmed supplements. Map the employer's needs to direct evidence, transferable evidence and genuine gaps.
3. Build one coherent reason to interview Rob. Make it visible in the profile, skills and selected experience rather than producing a keyword inventory.
4. Keep Education and formal historical titles factual. Preserve the distinction between personal delivery, team responsibility, domain familiarity and specialist ownership.
5. Draft four calibrated sections: About me, Skills, Experience and Education. Select roles and bullets for distinct evidence; do not make the document a comprehensive biography.
6. Save the result as `working/cv.md` and retain unresolved facts as concise notes rather than employer-facing caveats.

Do not force a page count by changing facts, shrinking typography or silently cutting reviewed content. Page fit belongs to the later PDF-production stage.
