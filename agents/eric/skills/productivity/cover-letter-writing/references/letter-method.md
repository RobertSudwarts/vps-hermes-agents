# Cover-letter drafting method

## Establish the case

Keep four things distinct: verified employer needs, confirmed candidate evidence, a proposed contribution, and Rob's stated personal motivation. A job advert proves only its stated needs; a proposed contribution is not a promised outcome. Ask a narrow question only if a missing motivation, relationship or example would materially change the letter. Otherwise draft a bounded case and flag the personal framing for review.

Privately identify: why this work is a sensible next piece of work; why Rob's evidenced combination is useful; and why that combination matters to this employer. Select one or two concrete evidence bridges rather than touring every employer or listing the CV's skills. Do not claim adjacent experience fulfils a specialist requirement it does not establish.

For financial-product roles, where supported, make the combined case clear: accountable business ownership and understanding of assets, investment strategy, trading, execution and risk, together with hands-on engineering. Do not reduce this to incidental finance exposure, nor claim years, product-management responsibility, outcomes or scope that the record does not support.

For an open application, propose a bounded contribution connected to verified work. If Rob asks for a short exploratory note, name the relevant area and invite discussion; do not invent a vacancy, team, approved remit or live activity.

## Draft

Default to 150–220 words in short paragraphs for a normal letter, unless the application gives a limit or Rob asks otherwise. For a deliberately light one-paragraph note, use roughly 60–90 words and return only that paragraph unless a salutation, sign-off or document is requested. Do not pad to fill a page.

Use direct, measured first-person prose. A useful shape is: name the role and why the match makes sense; connect a relevant example to the employer's work; explain the complementary experience or judgement; close with an invitation to discuss the contribution. Prefer concrete work, ownership and useful outcomes over adjectives.

Avoid generic enthusiasm, employer flattery, mission boilerplate, promises to transform the organisation, unexplained metrics and a chronological CV retelling. Applying does not prove admiration, a lifelong passion, reasons for leaving, salary aims or ambitions: do not invent these. Address a named recipient only when their identity and relevance are verified; otherwise use a neutral hiring-team salutation. Never guess an address.

Rob's accepted wording takes precedence. Preserve an accepted sentence or paragraph exactly unless he asks for a specific change. When editing his own text, improve grammar and remove tentative filler without replacing chosen financial substance with generic technical language. Treat any new factual detail as confirmation to retain with provenance, not permission to rewrite the CV or protected record.

## Review before showing the draft

- Is the argument specific enough that it could not be sent unchanged to another employer?
- Does it add interpretation and purpose rather than duplicate the CV?
- Is every factual claim supported, and are proposed contributions and personal motivation honestly labelled?
- Does it distinguish hands-on work, human validation, systematic AI evaluation, transferable experience and specialist expertise accurately?
- Does the tone sound capable and human rather than promotional or pleading?
- Is it consistent with the delivered CV while still grounding claims in the factual record?

Keep research citations, internal paths and candidacy caveats out of the employer-facing letter. Deliver the requested draft, not just advice about one.
